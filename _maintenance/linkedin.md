# LinkedIn archive

The /linkedin/ page uses _data/linkedin.json. Jekyll renders the data into HTML.
JavaScript adds search, topic filtering, sorting, and monthly chart readouts.
All posts are shown by default, with no pagination or post counter. Search and
topic filters show every matching post. The archive always combines all years;
legacy year query parameters are ignored and removed from the URL.
The post table shows reactions and publication year, ordered by reactions by
default. Post-level impressions remain in the source data but are not displayed;
the monthly impressions chart and account totals are unchanged.

The first snapshot was recorded on 18 October 2026 UTC. It includes 28 selected
posts from 2023 through 2026, not a complete activity archive. Titles and summaries
are editorial descriptions. Each title links to its original post.

## Source coverage

Two non-overlapping LinkedIn exports provide the analytics:

- 14 September to 31 December 2025: 915,534 impressions and 8,884 reactions.
- 1 January to 17 October 2026: 1,726,356 impressions and 16,667 reactions.

The combined chart has 14 monthly bars. September 2025 and October 2026 are
partial months. Combined impressions are 2,641,890; combined reactions are 25,551.
Unique members reached cannot be added across exports. Followers are shown only
as a current snapshot, not as historical year-end values.

The Scholar-style index table uses lifetime reactions on the archived posts,
not period-wide reactions or academic citations. The reaction h-index is the
largest h for which h archived posts each have at least h reactions. The i10-index
counts archived posts with at least 10 reactions. All covers the entire archive;
Since 2026 includes posts published in 2026 or later, regardless of when their
reactions occurred.
These are lower bounds for the full account because this is a selected archive.
The current 28-post snapshot has a reaction h-index of 27 and i10-index of 27.
Both indices and chart scales are regenerated by the importer and tested.

The requested 4 January to 31 December 2024 and 2025 ranges were tried separately.
LinkedIn rejected dates before its retention boundary; the first accepted date
was 14 September 2025. No older impressions or analytics totals were invented.

Eight older posts were checked through public LinkedIn pages. Their publication
dates and reaction totals came from the pages' SocialMediaPosting JSON-LD, not
from an inferred post-ID timestamp. Their public URLs are retained in the data.

## Refreshing the metrics

Download an XLSX content analytics export from the account owner's LinkedIn
analytics page. Select exactly one calendar year per export. Keep the XLSX files
outside the repository; they contain demographics that the website does not use.

Run the importer with Python and openpyxl:

    python3 scripts/import_linkedin_analytics.py /path/to/export.xlsx \
      --observed-at YYYY-MM-DD --reactions VERIFIED_PERIOD_TOTAL

The optional reactions value must be read from signed-in analytics for exactly
the exported dates. Engagements include other interactions and are not likes.
If the period changes without a verified reaction total, its previous reaction
total is cleared. Unchanged periods preserve the original reaction count and its
observation date.

The importer reads DISCOVERY, ENGAGEMENT, FOLLOWERS, and the impression ranking
in TOP POSTS. It checks dates and reconciles daily impressions with the total.
It joins posts by URL because the engagement and impression rankings differ.
It replaces only that year's snapshot; reimporting does not double-count it.
Combined metrics add disjoint periods only, rejecting overlap.

Per-post impression snapshots are retained by reporting year. A post absent
from a ranked export has an unknown count, not zero. The combined view adds
known counts and marks an incomplete sum with +. Unknown counts remain N/A.
Periods before a post's publication date do not create a missing contribution.
Per-post reactions are lifetime counts, checked separately from period totals.

Check each post and update reactions and reactions_observed_at manually. New
posts require a title, summary, topic, URL, and a source-verified publication date.
The export contains only a ranked subset, so absence does not mean no activity.

Validate and build:

    python3 -m unittest discover -s scripts -p 'test_linkedin_analytics.py'
    node --check js/linkedin.js
    bundle exec jekyll build
    python3 scripts/check_built_site.py

Review desktop and mobile views, the combined chart, legacy year links, empty
search results, sorting, and full-list visibility before publishing. There is no scheduled
LinkedIn scraping, browser session, token, or API credential in this page.
The existing weekly academic-metrics refresh does not update LinkedIn.
